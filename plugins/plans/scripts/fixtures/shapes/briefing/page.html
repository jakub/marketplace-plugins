Where the office bandwidth goes

The office moved 14.2 TB from July to September. Backups sent 46% of it, all at night. The busiest daytime hour used 58% of the line, so the line can stay as it is.

What to do

  1. Keep the 500 Mbit/s line. The busiest daytime hour used 290 Mbit/s down, 58% of the line.1 A faster line buys room that nobody uses yet.
  2. Keep backups at night. They fill 94% of the upload between 01:00 and 05:00, and nothing else runs then. In the day they would take most of the upload that video calls need.
  3. Cap the update cache at 200 GB. Updates pulled 1,900 GB, and 640 GB of that was packages fetched a second time after the cache dropped them.2
  4. Replace switch 2 before you trust its per-port numbers. It reset its counters four times this quarter. The totals on this page come from the router, so they don't depend on it.

By use

Backups move the most data, and almost all of it goes up, at night. Video calls set the daytime peak. A third of the updates' 1,900 GB is waste.

Coverage

Three sources cover the quarter, and none of them covers all of it. The router's hourly export stopped for nine days in August, until someone restarted it by hand.

The firewall log covers that gap, but it counts connections, not bytes. August's 1,290 GB for those days is an estimate from it.3 Its two exports overlap for 17 days, and the second one replaces the first there.

Switch 2 polled all quarter and reset its counters four times, each time at night.

By month

September moved the most, and its busiest hour, Tuesday 15 September at 10:00, set the quarter's peak. August is the only month with an estimate in it.

Unknowns

  • unknown How much of “Other” is the guest Wi-Fi. The firewall sorts traffic by port, and guests use the same ports as staff.
  • unknown Whether switch 2 loses counts at a reset or only starts them again. The router totals don't change either way.
  • unknown What the nine missing router days held, byte for byte. The 1,290 GB is an estimate.
  • partial Whether September's video-call peak is a trend or the first busy week after the break. One month can't tell, and October's numbers will.

Sources

Four data sources feed this page, and three notes say how the derived numbers were made.

Data

  1. Router export, traffic-2026q3.csv: bytes per device per hour, 83 of 92 days.
  2. Switch 2 counters, polled every five minutes per port, with four resets.
  3. Firewall log, two exports, one line per connection. It sorts traffic by use and fills the router's gap.
  4. Update cache log, cache-access.log: hits, misses and second fetches.

Notes

  1. A peak is the busiest whole hour, averaged over that hour. A one-second burst can be higher.
  2. Counted from the update cache's own log: a second fetch is a package the cache had already fetched and then dropped.
  3. The firewall's connection counts for 13 to 21 August, times the bytes per connection of the two weeks either side.
